## Without skill: full answer

> You probably don't need Velero for the cluster config, but you definitely need backups for the data. ArgoCD can rebuild what git describes. It can't rebuild PostgreSQL rows or wiki uploads, because those live only on your PersistentVolumes. If you lose the cluster or its storage without backups, you get back a clean, empty wiki.
>
> ## What git covers and what it doesn't
>
> | Thing | Rebuilt from git? |
> |---|---|
> | Deployments, Services, Ingress, Helm releases | ✅ Yes |
> | CRDs and operators | ✅ Yes |
> | PostgreSQL data | ❌ No |
> | Wiki uploads (PVC) | ❌ No |
> | Secrets that aren't in git (manually created, or generated by operators) | ❌ No |
> | **Sealed Secrets / SOPS private key** (if you use either) | ❌ No. Lose it and you can't decrypt any secret in your repo |
> | cert-manager certificates | ⚠️ They get reissued, which is fine unless you hit Let's Encrypt rate limits |
>
> ## What I'd do instead of (or alongside) Velero
>
> **PostgreSQL: use backups built for Postgres, not volume snapshots.**
> - If you use CloudNativePG, turn on its Barman backups to S3/object storage. You get base backups plus WAL archiving, which means point-in-time recovery.
> - Otherwise, use pgBackRest or WAL-G, or at the very least a `pg_dump` CronJob that ships the dump offsite.
> - Velero snapshots a running database's volume at an arbitrary moment. Postgres usually recovers from that, but it's the weakest option you have.
>
> **Wiki uploads: back up the files somewhere outside the cluster.**
> - Velero's file-system backup (Kopia) handles this well.
> - Other options: a restic/kopia CronJob, or configure the wiki to store uploads in S3 directly so the PVC stops mattering.
>
> **The encryption key.** If you use Sealed Secrets, export the controller's key once and store it outside the cluster, for example in a password manager.
>
> ## When Velero is worth running
>
> - You have several stateful PVCs and want one tool to back them all up.
> - You want a single command that restores namespace plus data.
> - You want to migrate between clusters.
>
> If your only stateful apps are Postgres and the wiki, Postgres-native backups plus a small file backup for uploads is simpler and gives better Postgres recovery than Velero would.
>
> ## Test your restores
>
> Backups only count once you've restored from them. Occasionally, bring up a new cluster from git, restore the database and uploads, and check that the wiki works. That's also how you'll find anything that's quietly missing from git.
>
> If you tell me how Postgres is deployed (CNPG, Bitnami chart, Zalando, etc.) and which wiki you run, I can sketch the exact backup manifests.

## With skill: full answer

> Yes, you need backups. Whether Velero is the right tool is a separate question.
>
> **Git only holds what the cluster should look like, not the data inside it.** ArgoCD can recreate your PostgreSQL StatefulSet, the wiki Deployment and their PVCs from git. But the new volumes will be empty. After a rebuild you'd have a working Postgres with no databases and a wiki with no pages or uploads.
>
> **What a rebuild from git won't bring back:**
>
> | Item | Rebuilt from git? | Needs backup |
> |---|---|---|
> | Manifests, Helm values, app config | ✅ | — |
> | PostgreSQL data | ❌ | **Yes** |
> | Wiki uploads (on a PV) | ❌ | **Yes** |
> | Wiki pages/metadata | ❌ (probably stored in Postgres) | Covered by the DB backup |
> | Secrets not stored in git (sealed-secrets/SOPS private key, ArgoCD repo creds, anything created with `kubectl` by hand) | ❌ | **Yes**, and this one is easy to miss. If you lose the sealed-secrets key, none of the SealedSecrets in git can be decrypted |
> | cert-manager ACME account keys, objects that operators create at runtime | ❌ | Usually fine to regenerate; check each one |
> | etcd/PKI (only if you run your own control plane) | ❌ | Only matters for in-place recovery. A git rebuild skips it |
>
> **Recommended setup (Velero is only one piece):**
>
> 1. **PostgreSQL: use the database's own backup tools, not Velero on its own.** Velero's file-system backup copies files one by one while the database is running, so the copy can be inconsistent. A CSI snapshot is only crash-consistent. Better options:
>    - If you use CloudNativePG or a similar operator, turn on its built-in backups to S3 (base backups plus WAL archiving). This also gives you point-in-time recovery.
>    - Otherwise, a `pg_dump` CronJob that ships the dump to off-cluster object storage.
> 2. **Wiki uploads PVC:** Velero works well here, using a CSI snapshot if your storage supports it, otherwise file-system backup. A simple `restic`/`kopia` CronJob to S3 also works. If you use Velero's file-system backup in opt-in mode, you have to annotate the volume. An unannotated volume is silently skipped.
> 3. **Secrets that aren't in git:** export the sealed-secrets/SOPS key once, keep it somewhere outside the cluster (like a password manager), and write down how to restore it. You could also let Velero take a daily backup of the `kube-system` and `argocd` namespaces. Velero backups store Secrets in plaintext, so lock down and encrypt the bucket.
> 4. **Store backups off the cluster.** A backup on the same cluster or storage as the data is lost along with it.
> 5. **Test a restore.** Restore Postgres into a scratch namespace every so often and check that the wiki actually shows its content. Also alert when the last successful backup gets too old. Until you've restored from a backup, you don't know it works.
>
> **So do you need Velero itself?** For this setup, not strictly. Postgres backups from its operator or `pg_dump`, a backup job for the uploads volume, and an offline copy of your secrets key would cover everything. Velero is worth adding if you want one tool with schedules and restores for the uploads PV and the leftover cluster objects. It doesn't replace proper Postgres backups.
>
> Three answers would let me turn this into a concrete design:
> - How is Postgres deployed: CloudNativePG, the Bitnami chart, or plain manifests?
> - What's your storage backend (Longhorn, Ceph, local-path, cloud disks), and does it support CSI snapshots?
> - Is the control plane self-hosted (k3s, kubeadm, Talos) or managed? Also, how much data can you afford to lose: a day, or less?
